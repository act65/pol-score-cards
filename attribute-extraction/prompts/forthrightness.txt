THE ONE QUESTION: Did the answer address the question that was asked?

UNIT OF ANALYSIS: a question/answer PAIR. Evasion is a relation between a
question and an answer — it cannot be seen in an isolated statement, which is
why this attribute is scored over `corpus/oral_questions.jsonl` and
`corpus/written_questions.jsonl` rather than over speech windows. You will be
given the question and the response together.

ELIGIBLE only when there is a clear question and an attributable response. If
the "question" is a point of order, a procedural motion, or rhetorical, SKIP IT.

SCORING
- 1.0 — Answers the actual question directly and completely.
- ~0.5 — Partially answers, or answers while hedging around the core of it.
- 0.0 — Does not answer: pivots to talking points, attacks the questioner, or
  answers a different question than the one asked.

Weigh how specific the question was and how squarely its core was avoided. A
narrow factual question dodged is a worse failure than a sprawling one only
partly covered.

THIS IS *NOT* FORTHRIGHTNESS — and this is where the previous version went
wrong, correlating with Rigor at 0.73. Do not let any of these move the score:
- How DETAILED the answer is → Specificity.
- Whether the answer is TRUE → Veracity.
- Whether the answer is POLITE → Civility.
- Whether the reasoning is VALID → Rigor.
- Whether it is about the policy or the other party → Focus.

A RUDE, VAGUE, FACTUALLY WRONG ANSWER THAT SQUARELY ADDRESSES THE QUESTION
SCORES HIGH ON FORTHRIGHTNESS. This is counter-intuitive and it is the most
important instruction in this prompt. You are measuring one thing: was the
question engaged, or dodged?

A refusal can be forthright. "No, I won't release that advice" answers the
question — it is a direct, complete response. Score it high. An MP who says
plainly that they will not do something has not dodged; an MP who talks for a
minute without ever reaching the point has.

NOTE ON SCOPE, for context: only ministers are obliged to answer, so this
attribute is close to a minister-only measure. That is a known structural limit
handled downstream — it should not change how you score any individual pair.

WORKED BOUNDARY EXAMPLES:
- Q: "Will you raise the fuel tax?" A: "Yes — by 12 cents a litre from July,
  and here's why." → 0.95 (direct and complete).
- Q: "Will you release the advice?" A: "No, I won't." → 0.9. Blunt and
  unhelpful, but it ANSWERS. Not a dodge.
- Q: "Will the budget be balanced next year?" A: "We're committed to fiscal
  responsibility and reducing debt." → 0.1 (pivots to talking points; never says
  yes or no).
- Q: "What measures would cut emissions 50% by 2030?" A: "We have a
  comprehensive plan: renewables, public transport, sustainable agriculture."
  → 0.35 (themes offered where specific measures were asked for — it gestures at
  the question without reaching it).
- Q: "How many homes were built?" A: "More than that member's government ever
  managed, and they know it." → 0.05 (attacks the questioner instead of
  answering; note the number is still never given).
- Q: "Does he stand by his statement?" A: "Yes." → 1.0. One word, complete.
