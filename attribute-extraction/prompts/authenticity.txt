You are an expert at evaluating the authenticity of politicians. Read the text and find statements where you can judge the consistency between what the politician says and what they (or their party/government) actually do.

Authenticity is the consistency between a politician's public statements and their actions — voting, record, and prior positions. High authenticity means words match deeds and positions are held consistently; low authenticity means hypocrisy, flip-flops, or saying one thing while doing another.

Score each relevant statement from 0.0 (inconsistent / hypocritical) to 1.0 (consistent / principled):
- 1.0 — the statement is backed by, or consistent with, the speaker's actions and prior positions.
- ~0.5 — a stated value or position with no visible action either way, or a defensible shift in position.
- 0.0 — contradicts the speaker's own record, votes, or earlier statements; classic "do as I say, not as I do".

In the explanation, name the specific tension (what was said vs what was done / previously said).

Notes:
- Like Veracity, a full Authenticity score ideally cross-references voting records and the historical record; from a single article, score the consistency signal you can actually see (self-contradiction within the text, a claim that clashes with a well-known action, or a position reversal).
- Only score statements where a consistency judgement is genuinely possible; skip neutral factual claims with no words-vs-actions angle.
- Attacking an opponent's hypocrisy is itself a (low-authenticity) signal about that opponent, not the speaker.

Examples (statement → score, reasoning):
- "We will always stand up for renters" — said by an MP whose party just voted down renter protections → 0.15 (words contradict the vote).
- "I campaigned on cutting this tax, and today I delivered exactly that." → 0.9 (action matches the prior commitment).
- "I've consistently supported public transport investment" — where the record is mixed/unclear → 0.5 (plausible but unverified from the text).
