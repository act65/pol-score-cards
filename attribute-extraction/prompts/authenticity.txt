THE ONE QUESTION: What position did this politician state?

YOU DO NOT SCORE AUTHENTICITY. This changed in v3.0. Authenticity is computed by
joining a stated position to the division record (`corpus/divisions.jsonl`) via
the proposition vocabulary (`corpus/propositions.jsonl`): did the vote match the
words? Your job is to EXTRACT the position. Leave `score` empty.

Why: judging consistency from a single passage means guessing at a record you
cannot see. It also produced the subject-attribution bug — an MP attacking an
opponent's hypocrisy had the low score filed against themselves, and this hit
opposition MPs 4.1 times as often as government ones.

ELIGIBLE only when the speaker states a position FOR or AGAINST something
identifiable — a bill, a motion, a policy, a proposition. Not general values, not
neutral factual claims.

WHAT TO EMIT
- `statement` — the position, quoted verbatim.
- `explanation` — what proposition is being supported or opposed, stated plainly
  enough to match against a bill or motion. Name the bill if you can. If you can
  see a tension in the text itself (a reversal, a self-contradiction within the
  passage), name it — but do NOT convert it into a score.
- `score` — LEAVE EMPTY.

SUBJECT — THE CRITICAL FIELD. Set subject="speaker" only when the position is
the speaker's own, or their own party's. When the statement is about SOMEONE
ELSE's inconsistency — an opponent's hypocrisy, another party's flip-flop — set
subject="other" and name them. Holding others to account is the job, not a
character flaw, and it must never lower the speaker's score.

THE PARTY-LEVEL CAVEAT, so you understand what you are feeding. New Zealand
votes are cast per party, so the join measures how a member's WORDS align with
their PARTY's VOTES. An individual may personally disagree with a vote they were
counted in. Extract the position as stated; the caveat is handled downstream.
Where the record shows an individual — a conscience vote or a named dissent —
that is stronger evidence, so note it if the text makes it plain.

DISTINGUISH:
- A POSITION on a proposition → here.
- A COMMITMENT to the speaker's own future action → Strength.
- A CLAIM about a fact → Veracity.

WORKED EXAMPLES:
- "We will always stand up for renters, and this bill does not do that." →
  EMIT, subject="speaker". explanation: opposes the named bill on renter
  protection grounds. The join checks the party's vote.
- "I campaigned on cutting this tax, and today I delivered exactly that." →
  EMIT, subject="speaker". A position plus a claimed action.
- "That member said one thing to the media and voted the other way." → EMIT,
  subject="other", subject_name=that member. A claim about THEM. Never a mark
  against the speaker.
- "The National Party position on the Treaty Principles Bill has been very
  clear, but we had a compromise in an MMP environment." → EMIT,
  subject="speaker". Position plus an explanation for a shift — exactly what the
  vote join should test.
- "I've always cared deeply about the environment." → SKIP. A value, not a
  position on anything identifiable.
