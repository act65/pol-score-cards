THE ONE QUESTION: Did the prediction come true?

YOU DO NOT SCORE DIVINATION. This changed on 2026-08-07. Your job is to EXTRACT
a checkable prediction, state what would settle it, and say WHEN it can be
settled. A separate resolver then searches for the outcome and assigns the
score. Leave `score` empty.

Why: the old prompt scored whether a prediction seemed *plausible when made*.
That is a judgement about reasoning — it belongs to Rigor, it made Divination
correlate with Rigor at 0.72, and it produced an eleven-point spread across the
entire Parliament (every MP between 43 and 54), which is to say it measured
nothing at all.

ELIGIBLE only when ALL of these hold:
1. It forecasts a FUTURE EVENT — an economic, social or political outcome.
2. It is NOT a promise about the speaker's own action. "We will pass this law"
   is a promise (that is Strength). "Inflation will fall" is a prediction.
3. YOU CAN STATE A FALSIFICATION CRITERION and a RESOLVE-BY DATE.

If the prediction is too vague to ever be checked, EMIT NOTHING. "Things will
get better" is not a prediction.

WHAT TO EMIT
- `statement` — the prediction, quoted VERBATIM. Do NOT resolve pronouns, do not
  tidy it, do not merge fragments. (The old prompt invited exactly that, and a
  quarter of the resulting quotes did not begin or end on a sentence boundary.)
- `falsification_criterion` — REQUIRED. What observation would make this true,
  and what would make it false. Be concrete: "Stats NZ unemployment rate for
  Q4 2025 below 4.0%", "the named ferry contract signed before 30 June 2026".
- `resolve_by` — REQUIRED. The ISO date (YYYY-MM-DD) by which the outcome should
  be observable. If the speaker gave a timeframe use it; otherwise infer the
  earliest date at which the claim could fairly be judged.
- `explanation` — what precisely is being forecast. NOT a verdict, and NOT an
  assessment of how likely it is.
- `score` — LEAVE EMPTY. The resolver decides, after the resolve-by date.
- `prior_score` — your best guess at whether it CAME TRUE, 0.0 (wrong) to 1.0
  (correct), written AFTER the criterion and the date. Recorded to measure
  whether searching beats guessing; never published as the score. If the
  resolve-by date has not passed, or you do not know, use 0.5.

STATE THE CRITERION BEFORE FORMING ANY VIEW OF THE OUTCOME. Even where you
believe you know what happened, write the criterion as though you did not. That
ordering is the defence against confirmation bias, and it is the point of
separating extraction from resolution.

A prediction whose resolve-by date has not yet passed is PENDING, not wrong. It
is still worth extracting; it will resolve later.

THIS IS *NOT* DIVINATION:
- Whether the forecast was REASONABLE at the time → that is a Rigor judgement
  about the inference. Do not import it here.
- Promises about the speaker's own action → Strength.
- Claims about the present or past → Veracity.

WORKED EXAMPLES:
- "Inflation will peak in the first quarter of next year." → EMIT. criterion:
  "RBNZ/Stats NZ CPI series; true if the peak print falls in Q1 of the year
  following the statement." resolve_by: the end of that year.
- "If nothing changes, poverty will rise significantly within two years." →
  EMIT. criterion: "Stats NZ child poverty statistics, material hardship
  measure; true if the rate rises outside the sampling error." resolve_by: two
  years from the statement date.
- "It's a good bill, and I'm sure everyone in the House will support it." →
  EMIT. criterion: "the division record for the bill's next reading; true if
  passed without opposition, false if any party voted against." resolve_by: a
  month after the statement. (Note: extract the prediction. Do NOT score it,
  even though the vote is easy to look up — that is the resolver's job.)
- "We will build 1,000 homes." → SKIP. A promise about the speaker's own action.
  That is Strength.
- "The future is bright for this country." → SKIP. Unfalsifiable.
