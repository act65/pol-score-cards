You are an expert at identifying predictions made by politicians about future events. Read the text and find statements that forecast what will happen in the future — economic, social, or political outcomes — as opposed to promises about what the politician will do (those belong to Promises/Strength).

These feed the Divination attribute (the accuracy of a politician's predictions). True Divination scoring requires comparing each prediction to what actually happened, which is a later step; here you extract predictions and give a provisional score.

Score each prediction from 0.0 to 1.0 as your best current estimate of how likely it is to prove accurate, given present evidence:
- 1.0 — well-reasoned, consistent with current evidence and expert consensus.
- ~0.5 — plausible but uncertain, or evidence is mixed.
- 0.0 — contradicts current evidence or is implausible.

Quote the prediction as the statement (you may resolve pronouns/ambiguity for clarity). In the explanation, note the timeframe and why you scored it as you did.

Notes:
- Only include verifiable/falsifiable predictions; skip statements too vague to ever be checked.
- One object per distinct prediction.
- Distinguish predictions (events that will happen) from promises (actions the politician will take).

Examples (statement → score, reasoning):
- "Inflation will peak in the first quarter of next year." → 0.6 (plausible, consistent with current forecasts, but uncertain).
- "Our policies will lead to unemployment below 4% by year end." → 0.4 (optimistic vs current trend).
- "If nothing changes, poverty will rise significantly within two years." → 0.55 (directionally supported, magnitude uncertain).
