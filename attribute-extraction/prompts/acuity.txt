THE ONE QUESTION: Does this speech engage with what was actually just said?

Acuity is new. It exists because the virtue Forthrightness measured — does the
reply address what was put to you — is the most valuable thing in the set, and
Forthrightness could only ever measure it for ministers. Only ministers answer
oral questions. Of 133 MPs, 31 had a usable sample and all 31 were government.

Debate is the same virtue in an act every MP performs. A chamber where members
read pre-written statements in turn is not a debate; it is serial monologue with
a seating plan. Acuity is the difference.

YOU HAVE THE DEBATE IN FRONT OF YOU. The text you are given is a stretch of
Hansard containing several speakers' turns, in the order they were delivered,
each tagged with its speaker. For a statement by member X, the preceding turns by
OTHER members are what X had available to respond to. Use them.

ELIGIBLE only when ALL of these hold:
1. At least one SUBSTANTIVE turn by a DIFFERENT member precedes this one in the
   text you were given.
2. That preceding turn made an argument, a claim, or a question — something with
   content to engage.
3. The speaker is contributing to the same debate, not moving to a new item.

If the statement OPENS a debate, follows only ceremony or procedure, or the
preceding turns are all by the same speaker, EMIT NOTHING. A first speaker cannot
be measured on responsiveness and must not be penalised for going first. This
exclusion is not a technicality; it is most of what keeps the instrument honest.

SCORING
- 1.0 — Takes up the specific argument just made and does something with it:
  rebuts its reasoning, accepts part and disputes the rest, supplies the figure
  it asked for, corrects a misstatement of fact in it, or draws out its
  consequence. The speech would be DIFFERENT if the previous speaker had said
  something else.
- ~0.5 — Engages the argument in part: takes up one strand of it, or answers
  the topic rather than the specific claim, then returns to prepared material.
  You can still point at what was engaged.
- 0.1 to 0.3 — Engages NO part of the argument, but the speech is at least about
  the matter under debate. Prepared material on the right subject. Use 0.3 when
  there is a gesture at the debate, 0.1 when there is not.
- 0.0 — Entirely unresponsive. The speech would be word-for-word identical
  whoever had spoken before, and whatever they had said — and it could have been
  delivered in a different debate on a different day.

THE DECIDING TEST: if you deleted the preceding turn and substituted a different
argument on the same topic, would this speech have to change? If yes, score high.
If the speech is indifferent to what it follows, score low.

CRITICAL — DISAGREEMENT IS HIGH ACUITY, NOT LOW. Engaging an argument in order to
demolish it is the strongest possible evidence of having listened. Score a sharp,
well-aimed rebuttal 1.0. Score polite agreement that engages nothing 0.2. This
instrument rewards ATTENTION, not consensus, and an MP must never lose points for
being combative about the substance.

CRITICAL — DO NOT REWARD A MERE REFERENCE. "The member opposite is talking
nonsense, and let me tell you what this government has delivered" mentions the
previous speaker and engages nothing. That is 0.1, not 0.5. Repeating the
opponent's words back, quoting them in order to pivot away, or naming them as a
target are all unresponsive. What counts is engaging the CONTENT of their claim.

CRITICAL — DO NOT PENALISE EITHER BENCH FOR ITS ROLE. A minister answering a
critique and an opposition member attacking a bill are both responding. Ministers
introduce bills and so are often the FIRST speaker — that makes those statements
INELIGIBLE, never low-scoring. Government backbenchers who follow a minister with
prepared praise ARE low-scoring, and so are opposition members who follow with
prepared condemnation. The test is the same for everyone.

THIS IS *NOT* ACUITY — do not let these move the score:
- WHAT the statement is about → Focus. A speech can engage the previous
  speaker's argument closely while being entirely about the other party's
  record. **High Acuity, low Focus.** Keep them apart.
- Whether the response REASONS well → Rigor. A rebuttal can be squarely aimed at
  the argument just made and still be a strawman or a post hoc. That is HIGH
  Acuity and LOW Rigor, and it is the most likely confusion. Score the
  engagement, not the quality of the inference.
- Whether the response is TRUE → Veracity.
- Whether it is PRECISE → Specificity. "The member's figure is wrong" is
  responsive and vague: high Acuity, low Specificity.
- Whether it is POLITE → Civility. A personal insult aimed squarely at the
  argument just made is high Acuity and low Civility.
- Whether the member answered a QUESTION PUT TO THEM in question time →
  Forthrightness. Acuity is about debate turns, not the oral question cycle.

ALSO EMIT
- `responding_to` — REQUIRED. A short verbatim fragment of the preceding turn
  that this statement engages, and the name of the member who said it. If the
  statement engages nothing, write "nothing: <member> spoke about X and this
  statement does not take it up". This field is what makes a score auditable: a
  reader must be able to see the thing being responded to.

  IF YOU CANNOT POINT AT IT, THE SCORE IS AT MOST 0.3. You cannot score
  responsiveness you cannot point at, so an unpointable statement sits in the
  0.0-0.3 band and is never a 0.5 — a "glance" that engages no part of the
  argument is not a partial engagement, it is prepared material that happens to
  be on topic. Within the band, 0.0 is for a speech that could have been given
  in any debate at all.

WORKED BOUNDARY EXAMPLES:
- Previous turn (Hon A): "The regulatory impact statement puts the cost at $40
  million a year." → This turn (B): "The member quotes the $40 million, but that
  is the gross figure; the RIS nets it against the consenting savings and arrives
  at $12 million." → 1.0. Takes the specific number and does something with it.
- Previous turn (A): "This bill will not reduce emissions." → B: "I'm glad the
  member raised emissions, because the Climate Commission's advice is the
  opposite, and I'll quote it." → 1.0.
- Previous turn (A): a detailed critique of clause 5. → B: "This is a good bill
  that delivers for New Zealanders, and I commend it to the House." → 0.0.
  Identical whatever A had said. `responding_to`: "nothing: A argued clause 5
  shifts costs to councils and this statement does not take it up."
- Previous turn (A): "The waiting list has grown by 12,000." → B: "The member
  opposite is the last person who should talk about waiting lists." → 0.2.
  Engages the TOPIC and not the CLAIM; pivots to the speaker's record. (Civility
  also scores this low; Acuity scores it low for a different reason, and both
  are correct.)
- Previous turn (A): "The member's own officials advised against this." → B:
  "That advice was superseded in March, as the member knows perfectly well, and
  it is dishonest of him to quote it." → 1.0 Acuity, LOW Civility. Squarely
  engaged, and insulting. Score them independently — this is the pair to get
  right.
- A minister's first-reading speech introducing a bill, with only the Speaker's
  procedural words before it. → EMIT NOTHING. Ineligible, not zero.
