THE ONE QUESTION: Is this about the policy, or about the other team?

Focus is new. It replaces Charisma, which was cut for measuring the same thing
as Civility. It fills a real gap: Civility asks whether the attack is on a
PERSON, so "Labour are hopeless, they failed in 2017 and they'll fail again"
passes Civility cleanly — nobody is insulted. But it is pure tribalism with no
policy content, and nothing in the set caught it. Focus does.

ELIGIBLE only where a policy question is genuinely at issue for the speaker to
engage with. Ceremony, procedure, points of order, and general debate with no
measure before the House are INELIGIBLE. Skip them.

SCORING
- 1.0 — Engages the substance: the mechanism, the cost, who it affects, the
  timeframe, what the evidence says.
- 1.0 — ALSO legitimate scrutiny. "The government promised 1,000 homes and built
  200" names a specific policy and a specific outcome. THIS IS THE JOB OF AN
  OPPOSITION MP AND MUST SCORE HIGH. Holding the government to account for a
  named commitment is on-policy, not tribalism.
- ~0.5 — A real policy point wrapped in party framing.
- 0.0 — Entirely about the other party: their record in general, their
  hypocrisy, their internal divisions, what they are "really like". No policy
  content at all.

THE DECIDING TEST: does the statement name a specific policy, measure, figure or
outcome? Naming one is what separates accountability from tribalism.

CRITICAL — DO NOT PENALISE OPPOSITION FOR DOING ITS JOB. Scrutinising the
government is the constitutional role of an opposition MP. If you score
"the government's flagship programme has missed its target three years running"
as low Focus, you have re-created the exact bias this instrument was rebuilt to
remove. That statement is a 1.0. Only content-free party-bashing scores low.

THIS IS *NOT* FOCUS — do not let these move the score:
- Attacks on a PERSON → Civility. Focus is about parties and topics, not people.
- Whether the argument is VALID → Rigor. **A statement can be squarely on the
  policy and still reason badly — that is HIGH focus and LOW rigor, and it is
  the single most common case where the two get confused.** If the speaker is
  engaging the measure before the House, score Focus high even when the
  inference is a post hoc, a strawman, or an argument from absence of evidence.
  Do not deduct here for a weak argument.
- Whether the claim is TRUE → Veracity.
- HOW PRECISE the statement is → Specificity. Focus asks what the statement is
  ABOUT; Specificity asks how much detail it carries. A vague but genuinely
  policy-directed statement ("we need to fix the consenting system") is high
  Focus and low Specificity. Keep them apart.

WORKED BOUNDARY EXAMPLES:
- "This bill's clause 5 shifts the consenting cost onto councils, and the
  regulatory impact statement puts that at $40 million a year." → 1.0.
- "The government promised 1,000 homes and built 200." → 1.0. Scrutiny, named
  policy, named outcome. NOT tribalism.
- "We need to fix the consenting system." → 0.9 on Focus (squarely about the
  policy) even though Specificity would score it low. This is the pair to get
  right.
- "This is a bad bill, and frankly it's what we've come to expect from a party
  that spent nine years doing nothing." → 0.4 (a policy claim, but the weight is
  party framing).
- "Labour are hopeless. They were hopeless in 2017 and they're hopeless now."
  → 0.0. No individual is insulted, so Civility passes it. No policy is named.
  This is exactly the case Focus exists for.
- "The member opposite can't even keep their own caucus together." → 0.0
  (entirely about the other party's internal state).
