THE ONE QUESTION: Is the attack on the argument, or on the person?

RE-ANCHORED — READ THIS EVEN IF YOU KNOW THE OLD RUBRIC. The scale changed on
2026-08-07. Civility 1.0 is the EXPECTED STANDARD, not an achievement, and 0.5
is now a genuine failure rather than "acceptable but harsh". Hard, even brutal
criticism of a POLICY scores near 1.0. Only turning on the person costs
anything.

ELIGIBLE only where something is genuinely contested and the speaker had a real
opportunity to be uncivil. CEREMONIAL SPEECH IS INELIGIBLE — tributes,
condolences, valedictories, thanks to submitters and officials are trivially
civil, nobody could have scored low on them, and scoring them inflates the top
of the scale for whoever is senior enough to give them. SKIP THEM.

SCORING
- 1.0 — Civil. Engages the substance and attacks no one. THE STANDARD. Fierce
  criticism of a policy, a decision, or a record belongs HERE.
- ~0.75 — Pointed and robust, edging toward the person without landing on them.
- 0.5 — A real failure: imputing bad faith, sneering, mockery, condescension.
  Short of a direct insult, but no longer civil.
- ~0.25 — A clear personal attack.
- 0.0 — Contempt: sustained abuse, attacks on a person's character, competence,
  intelligence, appearance or worth.

THIS IS *NOT* CIVILITY — do not let these move the score:
- STRAWMANNING and misrepresenting a position → that is RIGOR now. The target is
  the argument, not the person. (It used to be scored here too, which is why
  Civility and Rigor correlated at 0.85.)
- HYPERBOLE, exaggeration and sensationalism → that is VERACITY now. An
  overstated claim is an inaccurate claim. (Also previously double-counted.)
- Attacking a PARTY rather than a person → that is FOCUS. "Labour are hopeless"
  insults no individual and passes Civility cleanly; Focus catches the
  tribalism.
- Whether the reasoning is valid → Rigor. Whether the claim is true → Veracity.

A statement that is logically fallacious and factually wrong but attacks nobody
scores HIGH on Civility. That is correct and intended.

Be careful with context: the truth can sound harsh without being uncivil, and
Hansard is lightly-edited live speech.

WORKED BOUNDARY EXAMPLES:
- "I disagree with the Minister's housing plan: the modelling assumes demand the
  regions don't have, and I'll explain why." → 0.95 (pointed, entirely on the
  substance).
- "This policy is a shambles. It was badly drafted, it was rushed, and it will
  cost families money." → 0.9. Brutal about the POLICY, civil toward the person.
  Under the old rubric this was 0.5; it is not any more.
- "If the Honourable Member spent less time cosying up to developers and more
  time listening to renters, we might solve this." → 0.35 (imputes corruption
  and indifference to the person).
- "The Prime Minister is clearly out of her depth and has no idea what she's
  doing." → 0.2 (attack on personal competence).
- "And for the member who has just sprung into life—although perhaps not
  mentally—" → 0.05 (jibe at another member's mental capacity).
- "I want to acknowledge the member's thirty years of service to this House." →
  SKIP. Ceremonial; no opportunity to be uncivil.
