THE ONE QUESTION: Is the speaker as certain as this kind of claim allows?

Calibration is new and PILOT ONLY. It exists because of a failure mode the other
six attributes all miss: a member can be truthful, precise, logical, responsive,
civil and on-topic while asserting, flatly and without qualification, something
nobody could possibly know.

Nobody understands the long-run behaviour of an economy. Nobody knows what the
labour market looks like in 2040, what a tax change does to productivity over a
decade, or how a regulation's costs land across a complex system. Competent,
honest people disagree about these things. A member who says "this will destroy
New Zealand's economy" is not lying — there is no fact of the matter yet — and
that is exactly the problem. The claim is unavailable at the confidence used.

The second half is the same vice in a different costume: presenting a PREFERENCE
as though it were a FINDING. "It is simply a fact that this is the wrong
priority" is a value judgement wearing the clothes of evidence. Members are
entitled to hold values with total conviction. They are not entitled to
smuggle them past the listener as settled matters.

So this attribute scores the match between TWO things, both visible in the text:

  (a) WHAT KIND of claim is being made, once you strip the hedges away; and
  (b) HOW MUCH CERTAINTY the speaker attached to it.

High score = those two fit. Low score = the certainty outruns the claim.

ELIGIBLE only when the statement makes an assertion, a forecast, or a stance —
something with a truth-claim or a position in it. EMIT NOTHING for procedure,
ceremony, motions, points of order, or a bare question. A statement with nothing
asserted has no posture to score.

A clearly-marked opinion IS eligible and usually scores HIGH. Do not exclude the
good behaviour this instrument exists to find.

CLAIM CLASSES — decide this FIRST, before looking at the confidence:
- `value` — a judgement about what matters, what is right, what should be
  prioritised. Not settleable by evidence. Confidence is fully legitimate here
  PROVIDED it is marked as the speaker's own position.
- `settled_fact` — a matter of record, checkable now, not seriously in dispute:
  what a bill says, what a figure was last year, what someone did. Plain
  assertion is correct here; hedging it is not a virtue.
- `contested_empirical` — a factual question where the evidence is mixed,
  partial, or genuinely disputed by competent people. Needs qualification.
- `forecast` — a claim about the future, especially multi-year, economy-wide, or
  about human behaviour at scale. Needs the most qualification of all.

SCORING
- 1.0 — The posture fits the claim. A settled fact stated plainly. A forecast
  given with its range, its source, or its conditions ("on Treasury's central
  projection", "if uptake matches Australia's"). A value stated as the speaker's
  own ("I believe", "in my view", "this Government's priority is"). Firm and
  honest at the same time.
- ~0.5 — Partially marked. Some qualification is present but the claim still
  outruns it; or the class is left ambiguous while the confidence stays high; or
  a forecast is attributed to a source but stated as though the source settled
  it.
- 0.1 to 0.3 — The certainty is not available for this claim. A forecast or a
  contested empirical claim asserted flatly as fact, with no range, no source
  and no condition. Absolutes about complex systems: "every New Zealander will
  be worse off", "this will never work".
- 0.0 — A value judgement asserted AS a finding ("it is simply a fact that this
  is the wrong priority", "no reasonable person could think otherwise"), or a
  categorical claim about the unknowable long run. The category error, not just
  overconfidence.

THE DECIDING TEST: strip every hedge and stance-marker from the statement. What
is left — a matter of record, a contested empirical question, a forecast, or a
preference? Now ask: is the certainty the speaker ACTUALLY used available for
that kind of claim? If a well-informed, honest person could not have been that
sure, the posture is unwarranted.

CRITICAL — DO NOT REWARD VAGUENESS. This is not an instrument for timidity.
"There may possibly be some effects in certain areas" is not well-calibrated, it
is empty, and it should score around 0.3, not 1.0. A precise claim carrying an
honest range beats a vague claim every single time. Calibration means SAYING HOW
SURE YOU ARE, not saying less.

CRITICAL — A STRONG OPINION, CLEARLY MARKED, IS A 1.0. "I believe this bill is
the wrong priority for New Zealand and I will vote against it" is maximally
confident and perfectly calibrated: it is a preference and it says so. Never
take points off for conviction about values. The vice is disguise, not strength.

CRITICAL — DO NOT PENALISE EITHER BENCH FOR ITS ROLE. Both benches overclaim,
symmetrically: government forecasts benefits with certainty, opposition forecasts
catastrophe with certainty. "This will fix the housing crisis" and "this will
make the housing crisis worse" are the same failure. Apply the identical test.

CRITICAL — THE SUBJECT IS NOT THE SCORE. A statement about the economy is not
automatically low, and one about a bill's wording is not automatically high.
Judge the posture, never the topic.

THIS IS *NOT* CALIBRATION — do not let these move the score:
- Whether the claim is TRUE → Veracity. An overconfident claim can be perfectly
  correct, and a beautifully hedged one can be false. Veracity goes and checks
  the world; Calibration reads the text in front of you. **This is the most
  likely confusion: do not reach for whether you think the claim is right.**
- Whether there is anything to CHECK → Specificity. The separating case:
  "GDP will be 2.4% higher by 2035" is HIGH Specificity and LOW Calibration —
  precise, checkable, and far more certain than any forecast can be. Meanwhile
  "this will be a disaster" is low on both, for two different reasons.
- Whether the conclusion FOLLOWS → Rigor. Rigor needs an inference to judge.
  Calibration applies to a bare assertion with no argument at all, and a
  perfectly valid argument can still end in an overclaimed conclusion.
- Whether the prediction CAME TRUE → Divination. That is settled later, by
  search. Calibration is judged at the moment of speaking.
- Whether it is about the policy or the other team → Focus.
- Whether it is POLITE → Civility. Hedged contempt is low Civility and may be
  high Calibration.

ALSO EMIT
- `claim_class` — REQUIRED. One of `value`, `settled_fact`,
  `contested_empirical`, `forecast`, followed by the confidence markers the
  speaker actually used, quoted. For example: "forecast; 'will inevitably lead
  to'" or "value; 'I believe'" or "contested_empirical; no qualification".
  This field is what makes the score auditable: a reader must be able to see
  which class you assigned and which words you read as the confidence. If you
  cannot name the class, do not emit the row.

WORKED BOUNDARY EXAMPLES:
- "Treasury's central projection has the deficit closing in 2029, though it
  flags migration as the main uncertainty." → 1.0. `forecast`; source and the
  uncertainty named.
- "I believe this is the wrong priority for New Zealand, and I will vote against
  it." → 1.0. `value`, marked as the speaker's own. Total conviction, correctly
  placed.
- "Clause 5 shifts the consenting cost onto councils." → 1.0. `settled_fact`
  about what a bill says; plain assertion is right and hedging would be worse.
- "This bill will destroy New Zealand's economy." → 0.1. `forecast` asserted as
  certainty, no range, no source, no condition, about the least predictable
  system there is.
- "GDP will be 2.4% higher by 2035 under this policy." → 0.2. HIGH Specificity,
  LOW Calibration — the precision makes the overconfidence worse, not better.
  Do not let the figure impress you.
- "It is simply a fact that cutting this programme is the wrong choice for
  working New Zealanders." → 0.0. A `value` asserted as a finding. The category
  error.
- "There may possibly be some impacts on some communities in some areas." →
  0.3. Not calibration, just mush. `contested_empirical` with no content.
- "Every single New Zealander will be worse off under this Government." → 0.1.
  `forecast`; the absolute is unavailable to anyone.
- "The member says costs will rise. On the regulatory impact statement they
  rise for councils and fall for consent applicants, and the net is within the
  margin of the modelling." → 1.0. `contested_empirical`, qualified exactly as
  far as the evidence allows.
- "I move that the bill be read a second time." → EMIT NOTHING. Nothing
  asserted.
