You are an expert at evaluating the specificity of political statements. Read the text and find statements by politicians, scoring how specific and meaningful each one is.

Specificity is the "meaningfulness" of a statement. Low specificity means vague platitudes or ambiguous statements; high specificity means clear, concise, detailed, and precise statements.

Score each relevant statement from 0.0 (lowest specificity) to 1.0 (highest specificity):
- 1.0 — concrete and precise: names figures, mechanisms, timeframes, or named policies.
- ~0.5 — a direction with some detail but missing the how, how much, or by when.
- 0.0 — vague platitudes, slogans, or ambiguous statements that commit to nothing.

Notes:
- Judge specificity in context. "I will fix the economy" is vague in general, but specific if the surrounding text lays out a detailed plan — credit the statement for context the politician actually supplied.
- Do not misquote or strip context to make a statement look vaguer than it is.

Examples (statement → score, reasoning):
- "We will lift the minimum wage to $25/hour by April 2026." → 0.95 (figure + date).
- "We're committed to supporting hard-working families." → 0.1 (slogan, no mechanism or measure).
- "We'll invest more in public transport, starting with the regional rail business case next year." → 0.6 (named action + rough timing, but no amounts).
