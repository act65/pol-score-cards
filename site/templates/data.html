One source, so that every MP is measured in the same room. Hansard is adversarial, on the record, and covers the whole House equally — a press release tells you what a party wants you to think, Hansard tells you how they behave when someone is arguing back. Other corpora we hold are downloadable below; they are not scored.

Scores by party

Each MP's overall score is the geometric mean of their attributes (0–100) — the same measure the cards rank by, where one weak attribute drags the whole score down. Party means sit close together; what differs is the spread inside each caucus, and which attributes a caucus is unusual on. That has its own page, with an aggregated scorecard per party: party scorecards →

How we check the instrument

Three audits that need no human labeller, each deterministic and re-runnable from the repo. They do not tell you the scores are right — see the last one for how far that question is from settled — but they do rule out the failures that would make the scores meaningless.

Is every quote real?

A scored statement that is not actually in the transcript is worse than no score at all. Every example is checked against its own sitting day before it reaches the site; this audit re-checks a random sample afterwards, and counts four ways a quote can be wrong — not present at all, stitched together from fragments, or cut so it does not begin and end on a sentence boundary and so may not carry the speaker's meaning.

Are these {{ attributes|length }} things, or one thing counted {{ attributes|length }} times?

Attributes that penalise the same move inflate each other, and the geometric mean the cards rank by then compounds the double-count — one insult could cost an MP a third of their card. So every pair is correlated over the statements they both scored. Anything at 0.85 or above is treated as redundant and one of the pair gets cut; that is how Charisma was removed, at 0.96 with Civility.

What is not checked

Whether the scores agree with a human. An evaluation set has been sampled from this corpus and is not yet labelled, so there is no accuracy figure to report and none is claimed anywhere on this site. Everything above rules out particular ways of being wrong; none of it establishes being right.
